GPR15 de novo design run

GPR15 is an orphan receptor with a single binder in ChEMBL, too few to seed a similarity screen. This run therefore took the de novo route: DrugCLIP retrieved hits against the pocket, fragment growing turned the best hit into leads, and block level editing optimized the three strongest binders against predicted liver injury.

Largest DILI reduction

Predicted DILI falls from 0.541 to 0.412, the largest reduction in the run that does not wreck another endpoint, and mutagenicity falls tenfold to 0.028. Binding improves from −10.0 to −10.3 kcal/mol, the only candidate to gain on its lead.

How the three leads moved

Each lead was edited one block at a time, and the best improvement carried into the next iteration. Lead A gained the most in a single step but had nothing left to give in iteration 2, so it stopped early. Lead C started lowest and finished highest.

What each stage returned

  • Hits (stage 1): 5 molecules from the DrugCLIP screen, 3 of them with a favorable predicted DILI profile. Only the best scoring hit was carried forward.
  • Leads (stage 3): 15 valid leads from 15 fragment proposals, binding from −7.7 to −10.0 kcal/mol against the hit's −8.1.
  • Candidates (stage 4): 7 molecules that beat their parent, out of 81 enumerated across six iterations. One was rejected for losing more than 1.0 kcal/mol of binding.

Block level optimization

Stage 4, three leads over two iterations each

Each lead was decomposed into two blocks, and the agent proposed one block substitution at a time with a written rationale. A candidate had to beat its parent's DILI score and keep its binding within 1.0 kcal/mol of the lead.

The candidate worth showing

Only three of the seven candidates reduce DILI by more than 0.05 against their lead, and two of those break something else: the molecule the run selected sends mutagenicity from 0.153 to 0.941, and another drops intestinal absorption from 0.986 to 0.621. This one is left. It cuts DILI by 0.129, the largest reduction that does not come at that kind of cost, and improves mutagenicity, absorption, brain penetration and binding at the same time. One block substitution separates it from lead A: the dimethylamino group becomes a morpholine.

Block replaced
[1*]c1ccc(CN(C)C)cc1C(O)c1ccccc1
Replacement
[1*]c1ccc(CN1CCOCC1)cc1C(O)c1ccccc1
Rationale
Replacing dimethylamino with morpholine increases polarity and lowers lipophilicity and basicity, often reducing liver accumulation and off target liabilities while maintaining a basic solubilizing side chain in a conservative single site edit.

Against per endpoint limits (DILI below 0.5, AMES below 0.3, hERG below 0.7, Pgp below 0.6, HIA above 0.8, and BBBP below 0.5 for a gut restricted target) it clears four of six, failing Pgp and missing BBBP by 0.012. No candidate in the run clears all six.

Hit to candidate

Every path through the pipeline

All seven candidates descend from the one hit that docked, through three of its fifteen leads. Fragment growing is where the binding was won: the hit docks at −8.1 kcal/mol and the three leads at −10.0, −9.4 and −9.4. Block editing then traded binding for predicted safety, holding each candidate inside the 1.0 kcal/mol allowance.
